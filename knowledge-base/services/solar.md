# Solar & Battery

## Overview

Design, permitting, and installation of rooftop solar and home battery storage. In-house crews (no subcontracted installs), Xcel Energy interconnection handled for you. We are deliberately conservative on production estimates — we'd rather beat the projection than defend it.

## Services & pricing

- Free solar assessment: shade analysis, roof condition check, bill review, production estimate.
- Rooftop solar system (6–10 kW typical): $14,000–$26,000 before incentives.
- Federal residential clean energy credit: 30% of system cost, applied at tax time.
- Battery storage (10–13.5 kWh, e.g. for outage backup + time-of-use): $9,000–$14,000 installed; batteries qualify for the 30% credit even without panels.
- EV-charger-ready and panel-upgrade options quoted alongside (see electrical service line).
- System monitoring included; production guarantee on year-one output.

## Process & timeline

Assessment → design + fixed quote → contract → permitting and Xcel interconnection application (3–6 weeks, the longest part) → install (1–2 days) → inspection → permission to operate. Total typical timeline: 6–10 weeks from signing.

## FAQs

**Is my roof good enough for solar?** Panels outlive shingles. If your roof has under 10 years of life left, we'll say so and quote the re-roof; roof + solar as one project earns the multi-trade discount and avoids paying $2,000–$4,000 later to remove and reinstall panels for a re-roof.

**What happens with net metering?** Xcel credits exported kWh against your usage. Rates and programs change; we model your savings on the current tariff and show the assumptions, not a best-case fantasy.

**Do batteries make sense without frequent outages?** Sometimes — time-of-use arbitrage and backup for medical equipment or home offices are the main cases. If the math is thin for you, we'll show that honestly.

**Lease, PPA, or buy?** We only sell owned systems (cash or financed). Leases and PPAs complicate home sales and usually deliver less lifetime value to the homeowner.
